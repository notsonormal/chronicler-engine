- On 2026-11-11, a scout resolved ticket 13 ([asset](../wayfinder-maps/long-term-memory-and-state/assets/base-memory-seam.md)). Six of its line references were right on a spot check at `4baba0a4`. - On 2026-11-11, the scout proposed the writer prompt as code. The coordinator recommended a fifth preset type, because every other model call takes its prompt from a preset. The owner chose the code (the smaller build).
- On 2026-11-11, a strong-implementer started ticket 14 in the main checkout on `long-term-memory-2`, with no worktree: the work records of this session were not committed, so a worktree would not hold the ticket files. - On 2026-11-12, ticket 14 was resolved (uncommitted, parent `4baba0a4`). Two strong-implementer sessions each reached about 200k of context: the build (29 min) and the review fixes (25 min). A build ticket of this size should be two tickets (the refactors, then the feature).
- On 2026-11-12, the review round found one defect that no test showed: a summary that the answer limit cut off was saved as good. The check trial had shown the growth of the text first. A trial before the code review gave the reviewers that evidence.
- On 2026-11-12, four wordings of the writer prompt ran in the memory test. No wording kept the size of a rewritten summary under control with `deepseek-v4.1-flash`. A wording with two sections made the model return the old memory unchanged (33 %). Two of three runs often gave the same text, so the tool's spread is understated.
- On 2026-11-12, the owner asked for a ticket about the size of the story memory. The evidence had been only in `tmp/`, and the run files of one wording were already deleted. The coordinator moved the writer calls and the memory-test runs into the map assets. Evidence for an open finding goes into the map assets on the day of the finding.
- On 2026-11-14, the owner could not tell from ticket 14 whether the result was right. The ticket held the build notes, two review rounds and two check rounds one after the other, and its early "Not done" and "Departures" lists were out of date. It also missed the changes of the second review round (the ceiling of the Connection, one history space for each writer call). The coordinator replaced those sections with one Answer that gives the final state at `chronicler-engine@a9801e4a`. A build ticket that closes needs its Answer rewritten as the final state, and the log of the rounds belongs in git history.
- On 2026-11-14, the coordinator advised the owner to park the story memory and to work on the story clock and the card text first. Its reasons: the baseline trial had no confirmed fault of a fact that left the history, and the story memory does not run before about Cycle 170 at the default history size. The owner rejected this. The owner's words: the long-term memory is the most important thing of the map; that it does not run in a short Game is its purpose, not a fault; and the summary method of the base "would work for a coding harness but doesn't work for the story generator". A coordinator must not rank the story memory below the other parts of the map, and must not count "it runs only in a long Game" against a memory design.
- On 2026-11-14, the owner said that the map had lost sight of where it wants to get in principle. Two reviewers read the research assets again in full ([re-read](../wayfinder-maps/long-term-memory-and-state/assets/research-reread-2026-11-14.md)). The finding: on 2026-11-11 the comparison of designs was replaced by "build a base, then change it", the base took the weakest form of the research (one rewritten summary), and nobody held the research against it afterwards. The memory test also shaped the design, because it scores only one text block.
- On 2026-11-14, the coordinator proposed three levels by age: the history, scene entries that never change, and one rewritten "story so far" for the oldest part. The owner accepted the scene entries and rejected the rewritten text: it is what the base has, and a second summary is a summary from a summary. The owner also said that the starting scenario is the most important thing for framing. Known at `a9801e4a`: the opening Narration is the scenario text of the World (`src/domain/model/utils/opening.rs`), it is only the first Message, and no other part of the prompt holds it. So it leaves at the first history cut. The rule from this: no part of the story memory is a text that a writer rewrites from its own earlier version.
- On 2026-11-14, the owner gave three more positions on the design. First: when the scene entries pass their space, the oldest entry leaves the prompt, and a memory view in the dashboard lets a person keep a scene in the prompt or edit an entry. The owner was not sure about a load of old entries by a key. Second: Character memories are still needed as a separate part. Third: the owner is not sure whether "world memories" are needed, as a way to set world states and facts apart from the scenes. The owner's concern: with these parts the loss of old scenes matters less, but the parts overlap and the whole is harder to reason about.
- On 2026-11-14, the owner agreed to the design, and the coordinator wrote it into the map. [Decide how the story memory keeps its size](../wayfinder-maps/long-term-memory-and-state/issues/15-story-memory-size.md) is resolved by a change of form, with no measurement, and its Answer holds the design. The map Notes hold the rules ("The memory in principle"). The next ticket is [Decide the form of the scene entries](../wayfinder-maps/long-term-memory-and-state/issues/16-scene-entries.md). The lesson: when a prototype ticket asks how to fix an effect, check first whether the form that causes the effect is the right one.
