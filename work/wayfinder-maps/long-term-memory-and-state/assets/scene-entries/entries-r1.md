## The opening, word for word

> Standing tall and commanding, Carla approaches Julian with a piercing gaze. She extends her arm for a firm handshake. "I'm Carla. I'll be your bodyguard both inside the Redmist Estate and wherever else you go. You'll notice me around frequently, so don't try to shake me off." She steps back, crossing her arms. "I need you to understand that your safety is my top priority. Everything I do is to keep you safe. Let's work together to make sure nothing compromises that. Firstly, I'll ask to borrow your phone. It won't take long."

### #524 to #532: Day 1, morning, The gravel drive outside the Redmist Estate gate

*Took part: Julian, Carla.*

Carla introduced herself as Julian's bodyguard, said his safety was her top priority, and asked to borrow his phone. Julian gave his name and asked what she would do with it. She said she would run a threat assessment — contacts, accounts, anything exposed — with nothing deleted and nothing leaving her hands, and he could watch. Julian kept the phone in his own hand and unlocked only what she needed. In the login history they found an entry from three days earlier, after Julian booked his train, from somewhere near the estate; Julian said he had told no one but his mother he was coming. Carla told him to lock the phone and keep the history, and explained the master key: one passcode Bernard held that opens every estate lock, to be handed to Julian in a witnessed procedure run by Jezebel. She said the login suggested someone knew the handoff was coming, and that Jezebel did not yet know about the login.

### #533 to #536: Day 1, morning, The gatehouse

*Took part: Julian, Carla.*

Walking to the gatehouse, Julian asked how the procedure could be compromised and whether Jezebel knew of the login. Carla said Jezebel did not know yet and that the login was theirs to hold for a day; she trusted Jezebel as an ally but considered the room, the witnesses and the lawyer Campion suspect until she had seen the procedure. Inside the gatehouse, which smelled of coffee and gun oil and had monitors showing the drive, the rear service road and the pier gate, Carla advised telling Jezebel straight that the key needed a review before the handoff, omitting why Julian had started caring that week, and letting her assume nerves. She said Jezebel's office was in the east wing and they left before anyone else could tell her about the login.

### #537 to #540: Day 1, morning, The marble hallway of the mansion

*Took part: Julian, Carla, Gabriella.*

Julian agreed to play the nervous heir and told Carla that if Jezebel's account of the procedure differed from what Carla remembered, that was Carla's cue to speak, not his; Carla agreed. In the marble hallway Gabriella, dressed in a lavender gown and pearls, stepped out and offered to show Julian the house, remarking on Carla trailing him. Julian declined evenly, saying he had business with Jezebel about finances and paperwork first and would walk the house on his own time. Gabriella mentioned a draft in the east wing, said she would be in the west parlor that afternoon, and went back upstairs. Carla told Julian there was no draft in the east wing and that it was either a warning or a lure; Julian said they would not bite today, and they continued toward Jezebel's office.

### #541 to #548: Day 1, morning, Jezebel's office in the east wing

*Took part: Julian, Carla, Jezebel.*

Julian knocked and Jezebel let him and Carla in. Julian asked to understand the accounts and foundations and to see the master-key handoff end to end before setting a new code, admitting he was new and worried. Jezebel described the procedure: the vault room off the old dispensary, lawyer Campion and herself as witnesses, a new code set on the spot from a sealed slip only Bernard had read, the slip destroyed afterwards, an hour in all. Asked who had told him, Julian said an old friend of Bernard's who knew how he thought. He then asked whether anyone else had ever handled the slip; Jezebel recalled a retired locksmith who serviced the mechanism four years earlier with the door propped open and the slip in hand, a visit she had signed off on. She asked what he feared in that room, and Julian revealed the login from three days ago, before he had told anyone in Islaport he was coming, and asked whom Bernard would have trusted with knowledge of the key. Jezebel named Bernard's dead brother Edmund, Campion the solicitor, and Marguerite, Bernard's nurse of his last two years, to whom he told things he would not tell Jezebel. She said Bernard wrote everything down in a log in the bottom drawer of his private desk in the study, which she had never opened, and that Julian might now have cause to.

## History cut at #569: Messages #549 to #568

### #549 to #552: Day 1, morning, Bernard's study and adjoining library in the Redmist house

*Took part: Julian, Jezebel, Carla.*

Julian said the login and the locksmith were cause enough to open Bernard's log, and asked Jezebel to come with him. She agreed, leaving Carla at the door, and warned that if the log was gone or rearranged they would go to Campion, not the police, to avoid scandal. In the study Jezebel unlocked the bottom drawer: it was empty. Julian checked for a false bottom and found nothing, and asked where else Bernard kept private things. Jezebel named the desk and the reading chair in the library, where Bernard kept a cushion he would not let anyone mend. In the library she lifted the cushion and found a small brass key with a tag stamped 'Hollis'.

### #553 to #556: Day 1, morning, The library of the Redmist house

*Took part: Julian, Jezebel, Carla.*

Julian examined the key and asked what the name meant and what lock it fitted. Jezebel said Hollis was Marguerite Hollis, widow of Bernard's dead brother Edmund, living on Marchand Street; Bernard sent her money every quarter and she never wrote back. Julian decided to visit her that day before word spread, and asked one or both women to come as witness. Jezebel described Marguerite as sixty-odd, once a hospital nurse, shunned by the family since Edmund's death twelve years ago; her house has a green garden gate. Carla warned Marchand Street was not to be walked blind and that they needed a reason for the visit. Jezebel advised saying only that Bernard left Julian the key with her name on it, and not mentioning the drawer.

### #557 to #560: Day 1, morning, The study of the Redmist house

*Took part: Julian, Jezebel, Carla.*

Julian agreed to the key story but said he would not lie if asked why now. He asked whether there was a record of the money sent to Marguerite. Jezebel found in a ledger quarterly payments for twelve years, a few thousand each, with a note on the last: 'For the boy's schooling.' She said Hollis had a son. Julian realised the boy would be Edmund's son and his own second cousin, and planned to tell Marguerite her son's name was in Bernard's hand. He asked who held the paying account and whether payments had stopped. Jezebel named Marchand Mutual bank and said she would ring them quietly before noon. Carla insisted on the carriage and on the agreed line about the key. Jezebel said the boy would be near eighteen.

### #561 to #562: Day 1, morning, The entrance hall of the Redmist house

*Took part: Julian, Jezebel, Carla.*

Julian said he would hear the boy out if he was there, and repeated that the key's story was settled: Bernard left it to him. Carla went out to have the carriage brought round and told him to stay where there was a wall at his back. Jezebel said she would ring Marchand Mutual from her office at once and tell him before he reached the gate if the draughts had stopped. She warned him that Marguerite had been a nurse and would read his face faster than his words, so whatever he told her he must mean.

### #563 to #566: Day 1, morning, The carriage, from the Redmist estate into the streets toward Marchand Street

*Took part: Julian, Carla.*

In the carriage Julian asked Carla how to handle a stranger's door. She advised: knock, say who he was and why he had come in one breath, then leave a gap and not fill the silence; if no one opened the door, that itself told them something. Julian asked what to do if the son answered. Carla said a young man of eighteen should get the cards down plain and early: the fact of the key and the fact of the money, and let him decide what to feel. A widow gets the gap, a son gets the truth before he asks. The carriage reached Marchand Street and slowed; Carla said the number was coming up on the left.

### #567 to #568: Day 1, morning, Marchand Street, at the green gate and blue door of Marguerite Hollis's house

*Took part: Julian, Carla; also Marguerite Hollis's son.*

Julian got down and told Carla to hang back a step so he would not arrive with a bodyguard crowding the threshold, but to stay close in case it turned ugly. He walked along the low wall counting numbers. The house had a chalky green gate with a sagging hinge, a neat working garden, and a recently polished brass knocker on a blue door. Before Julian reached the door it opened and a tall, thin young man stepped out with a pruning knife in his hand and dirt to the wrist, plainly Marguerite's son with something of the Redmist about the brow. He looked at Julian and then at Carla at the gate, shifted his grip on the knife, and asked if he could help him.

### #581 to #586: Day 1, morning, Marguerite Hollis's parlor and doorstep, Marchand Street

*Took part: Julian; also Marguerite Hollis, Tomas Hollis.*

Julian said he had not come to take anything back but to ask what the key opened. Marguerite said it was Edmund's strongbox, held by the bank after his death and not openable to her without a court; Bernard had promised to sort it before he fell too sick to leave the house, and the quarterly payments came without a line from him. She said the box belonged to Tomas. Julian said Tomas should turn the key and asked which bank held it. He warned Julian that his mother would spend the night arguing against the plan, out of being against hoping rather than against Julian.

### #587 to #590: Day 1, morning, The carriage returning to the Redmist estate, and the gate

### #597 to #604: Day 1, late afternoon, The Redmist estate, the study off the south corridor

*Took part: Julian, Carla; also Campion.*

Julian rang Campion, who said two matters could not wait. The master-key handoff had to happen that week, since a credential with a dead holder and no heir on record could be argued improperly transferred. And Bernard had added a six-line codicil in his last month, naming no one and granting nothing, but speaking of a trust established years before off the estate's books, whose beneficiaries were known only to Bernard and his banker, and saying no inquiry into it was suspicious. Campion said he had known nothing of it until that afternoon and had sat on it since three. Asked about the Draught, he said it meant the winter the Islaport harbor froze, nineteen-oh-five. He noted Bernard kept accounts at Marchand Mutual until it closed, then at Islaport Trust, and that an old account left open meant the old door still worked. The handoff was set for Friday at ten in the morning; Julian needed to bring only himself, and the vault wanted Campion's key and Jezebel's. Campion said the codicil had not left his desk and would next be discussed in Julian's presence, then hung up without goodbye. Carla, by the fire, asked whether Tomas should be told of the codicil before the meeting.

Julian decided Tomas must be told before the meeting, and better from the paper itself than from Julian's mouth, so the boy could see Bernard's hand. She reported that Halvorsen's clerk had rung: the lawyer would see Julian at eight in the morning at his office on King Street, for one hour. He asked her to take the boy out through the garden and the side gate by the kitchen, not the front drive, in case the gatekeeper's cousin was watching the street. Carla said she would not spend the evening persuading Tomas if his mother had talked him out of it, and warned Julian to eat before Halvorsen, leaving by half past seven.

Julian said he would send for Louise about supper and Lisette about clothes, not wanting to look like a man who had been chasing keys around the city. He asked Carla what to expect of Halvorsen as a man. Julian told her to eat before the cold walk with the codicil. Carla left toward the kitchens. ### #615 to #618: Day 1, evening, The Redmist estate, the corridor at the top of the stairs

*Took part: Julian, Louise.*

Julian met Louise at the corridor's end and told her he wanted something hot, honest and not heavy before his eight o'clock at a lawyer's office, and asked her to make what she would make if nobody were watching. Louise said that would be her mother's fish stew with mussels and cream, but for eight o'clock she would make fish, greens and a clear broth instead. She explained the kitchen kept a pot going from six in the morning till ten at night, with cold plates under cloth between, and asked him to ring down before seven if he would be out past ten and to tell her where he was going. Julian told her he would be at Halvorsen's office on King Street at eight and at the Islaport Trust at one, and that if he had not rung by seven he would want hot food on his return. Louise wrote it all in her notebook and said a pot would be on at six.

### #619 to #622: Day 1, night, The Redmist estate, the master quarters

*Took part: Julian, Carla.*

Julian went up to the master quarters, where the windows had been opened to air the room and a dark wool suit and spare shirt had been laid out on the bed for the morning. He shut the windows, changed in the lit dressing room, and sat at the writing table with paper and ink, undecided what to write. The pen dripped once onto the blotter and he blotted it with his thumb. Later a carriage turned in at the gate, and Carla knocked at his door to say the boy had come to the house himself, would not wait for morning or hand the codicil to her in the lane, and was in the library with the key.

### #623 to #630: Day 1, night, The Redmist estate, the library

*Took part: Julian, Carla; also Tomas.*

Julian had Carla walk ahead of him to the library, where Tomas stood by the reading chair with the key in his fist. Tomas said Carla had told him what the codicil said and he had decided not to sleep on it, and gave Julian a folded paper in his own hand recording what his father had told him about the winter of the Draught, corroborated by his mother. Julian read it and asked to hear it aloud. Tomas said the harbor froze, the boats came in half-starved, and Bernard came one night with a young doctor from the hospital and a list; the three spent till dawn deciding who was short of coal, who had children, and which boats were worth saving, and his father kept the accounts in a book, the only honest ledger of his life. Julian said the father's ledger was the paper they needed, alongside Bernard's journals, and asked whether it still existed. Tomas said it was in his mother's chest with the letters and death notices and she had never let him read it in twelve years. Julian told him to ask her as a son, not for a banker's desk, and said the strongbox would go to the Trust at one o'clock whether or not she gave up the book. Tomas left by the side gate.

### #631 to #634: Day 1, night, The Redmist estate, the library

*Took part: Julian, Carla.*

Julian asked Carla whether he had any right to want a widow's private ledger. Carla said he had a right to ask but not to have, that a widow's bedroom was still her bedroom, and advised him to want it, ask for it, and take no for an answer until he had a court or the son's word that the book was his father's to give, since a Redmist who took a book from a woman's chest would be talked about on the east side by breakfast. Julian agreed, put out the lamp, and said that in the morning before Halvorsen they would go to Marchand Street not for the book but to ask for it to her face. Carla said they would leave at half six and told him to take a no gently. She closed the library and went to her quarters.

### #635 to #637: Day 2, early morning, The Redmist estate hall, then Marchand Street

*Took part: Julian, Carla; also the driver.*

Julian came down before the hall lamps were up, in the dark suit, and checked the proper clock: half past five. He stood before a portrait of the Redmist who built the east wing until Carla came from the kitchen corridor with a lantern, saying the carriage was at the gate early. She remarked that the family had cut people open for four generations and none could keep a house in order. They drove through the dark city along Marchand Street, where one window in the house at the green gate was lit. Julian stepped down and came up to the gate but did not unlatch it, calling up to the lit window that he had come to ask a thing to her face and to hear the no to his face, and that nobody's arm needed twisting on his account.
