**Day 1, late afternoon into evening.** At the gate, Julian reads Jezebel's note twice, then has Carla read it. It says: the last draught cleared three weeks ago, after the funeral, before the will was read; sent on Bernard's standing, not Julian's; not collected since; the account was never formally closed and should not be closed yet. Halvorsen the lawyer will take the Trust but wants the will in hand and a client he can name without flinching; Jezebel rings him tonight. Her last part, for Julian: keep the account open — a closed account leaves a trail, an open one leaves a door. Carla: watch the account a week to learn who signs the draughts now. Halvorsen will want Julian before one o'clock, not at it; she'll arrange it.

Lisette brings Campion's card: he rang, wants a call back before evening; after six he takes the receiver off. Lisette leads them to a study off the south corridor — dark paneling, fire lit, decanter, telephone on the desk; Bernard's old sitting room, sorted by Carla that morning. Julian asks for cold water, no decanter; has Carla stay to listen. Campion, crisp: (1) the master-key handoff cannot wait past the week — Friday, ten in the morning, before offices open; bring only himself; the slip is in the vault, opened with Campion's key and Jezebel's. (2) Bernard altered his will in his last month — a codicil, six lines, names no one, grants nothing, speaks of a trust established years before, off the estate's books. The six lines: "To my solicitor, my accountant, and my grandson. The trust established in the winter of the year of the Draught belongs to no name that appears on the estate's books. Its beneficiaries are known only to me and to my banker. No inquiry into it is to be treated as suspicious. It is only Bernard tidying." Campion knew nothing of it until three o'clock that afternoon; the codicil has not left his desk. The Draught is the winter the harbor froze, nineteen-oh-five; the city counts years from it. "My banker," singular: Bernard kept accounts at Marchand Mutual until it closed, then Islaport Trust; the old door was meant to still work. The codicil stays quiet — Halvorsen hears it from Julian. Line clicks dead.

Julian and Carla agree: Tomas hears the codicil from paper, not Julian's voice, before they walk into the Trust. Carla takes the note tonight, has Tomas at the bench opposite the Trust at half past twelve; if he won't come out, that is an answer. Julian: if Tomas asks why Julian didn't bring it, tell the truth. Walk Tomas out by the kitchen side gate onto the back lane, hedges both sides, not the front drive. Halvorsen sees Julian at eight in the morning on King Street, one hour; leave by half seven; eat first. Carla on Halvorsen: old money, older manners; offers tea he doesn't want and judges how it's held; he measures, doesn't warm; tell the whole of Marchand; don't fill his silences; if he likes you he talks of next week. Carla goes to the kitchens; Julian tells her to eat first.

Lisette: Louise will come up about supper with her notebook and kitchen list. Julian orders clothes for morning — dark, sober, nothing that shines. Asks Lisette for house knowledge: the east corridor clock runs nine minutes fast, set by Bernard; staff keep kitchen time. A bell rings when harbor fog comes in — the study's west window doesn't shut true; on foggy nights it sounds like knocking; it is the wind. Louise's footsteps come up the corridor.
