**Day 1, evening into Day 2, before dawn.** Louise comes up with her notebook. Julian asks for honest, light food before an eight o'clock at a lawyer's; she offers fish stew, but for morning fish, greens, bone-broth, no cream. Kitchen keeps a pot six in the morning to ten at night, cold plates between; if out past ten, ring down before seven. He tells her: eight at Halvorsen's on King Street, one at the Islaport Trust; if he hasn't rung by seven, hot food on his return. She writes it in the book. He says she's the first to ask a question not about the will.

Upstairs, master quarters aired, sea air in, fire laid unlit, dark sober suit and spare shirt laid out. He shuts the windows, goes to the lit dressing room, comes back, sits at the writing table with pen and paper, writes nothing.

A carriage turns in at the gate. Carla knocks: the boy has come himself, won't take the codicil in the lane, is in the library with the key. Julian has Carla walk ahead, not beside. Tomas stands by the reading chair, key in one fist. He says he couldn't sleep on it longer; he's written out what his father told him before he died — the winter of the Draught, Bernard coming round with a young doctor and a list till dawn, who was short of coal, who had children, which boats to save; his father kept the accounts in a book, the only honest ledger of his life. Julian reads the paper, says the father's book is the paper they want, not this; Bernard's journals are on the shelves. Tomas: the book exists, in his mother's chest with letters and death notices; she's never let him read it in twelve years. He'll ask her tonight. Julian: ask as the son, not for the banker; the strongbox goes to the Trust at one o'clock book or no book. Tomas leaves by the side gate.

Julian asks Carla whether he has any right to want the book. Carla: a right to ask, not to have; a widow's bedroom is her bedroom; want it, ask, take no until he has better than wanting — a court, or the son's word. Julian agrees; plans to go to Marchand Street in the morning before Halvorsen, not for the book but to hear her reasons and her no to his face. Carla: half six, carriage round, take the no gently. House goes dark.

**Day 2, before dawn.** Julian comes down at half past five, dark suit, coat over arm, hat in hand; stands before the portrait of the surgeon who built the east wing. Carla comes with a lantern: carriage at the gate early. They drive through dark Marchand Street; one window lit at the green gate. Julian steps down, stops short of the gate in the lamplight, doesn't unlatch: he's come to ask to her face and hear the no to his face.
