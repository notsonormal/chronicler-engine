# Story Memory

**Day 1, morning.** Julian Redmist arrives at Redmist Estate, Islaport, after grandfather Bernard's death. Bodyguard Carla meets him at the gatehouse; login history shows an entry from three days ago — after he booked his train, before he told anyone but his mother. Someone knows his timeline. Keep the history, don't clear it.

**Master key:** one passcode Bernard set up years ago, opens every estate lock. Procedure: vault room off the old dispensary; lawyer Campion witnesses, Jezebel witnesses; new code set on the spot from a sealed slip only Bernard ever read, slip destroyed after. Takes about an hour. Carla: Jezebel is honest, an ally not a suspect, but the room, witnesses and lawyer are all suspect until checked. Plan: ask Jezebel straight, keep the login secret one day; if it leaks from her office, they'll know who's listening. Cover story: nervous heir wanting to see the locks work.

**East wing hallway:** Gabriella intercepts, lavender silk and pearls, offers a tour. Julian declines — business with Jezebel first. Gabriella warns of a "draft" in the east wing; Carla notes there is no draft — a warning or a lure. Plan: after Jezebel, find out what Gabriella doesn't want seen. Gabriella will be in the west parlor this afternoon.

**Jezebel's office, same morning.** Present: Julian, Carla, Jezebel. Four years ago a retired locksmith serviced the mechanism with the door propped open and the slip in hand; Jezebel signed off. Julian tells her about the login. Jezebel names confidants: Bernard's brother Edmund (dead twelve years), Campion (paperwork only), and Marguerite, Bernard's nurse for his last two years. Bernard wrote everything down — a log in the bottom drawer of his private desk in the study. She never opened it.

**Study, same morning.** Present: Julian, Carla (at door), Jezebel. Bottom drawer empty, no false bottom. Jezebel: Bernard kept things in two places — the desk and the reading chair in the library. Under the green cushion of the wing-backed chair: a small brass key tagged *Hollis* in Bernard's hand. Marguerite Hollis, Edmund's widow, lives on Marchand Street, green garden gate. Bernard sent her money every quarter for twelve years, quiet, she never wrote back. Julian decides to go today, before word spreads the drawer was opened.

**Ledger:** quarterly payments, modest, never missed, no stated purpose. Note by the last: "For the boy's schooling." There is a son, near eighteen, Julian's second cousin. Julian: cards down plain (key and money). Jezebel to find who holds the paying account and whether payments stopped. Bank: Marchand Mutual, near her house. Jezebel will ring before noon, quietly; Julian wants to know before they return whether the last payment cleared or sits uncollected.

**Line agreed:** Bernard left Julian the key with her name on it. Nothing about the drawer, nothing about the login. If she asks why now, tell the truth as far as it goes.

**Departure:** Carla calls for the carriage; five minutes, stay where there's a wall at your back. Jezebel: Marguerite was a nurse, will read his face faster than his words — mean it. Carla: knock, who you are, why you've come, in one breath; don't fill the silence. Julian carries the brass key and the bank slip.

**Gate, late morning.** Julian tells maid and gatekeeper plainly he's going to Marchand Street, east side, to call on a widow of the family name; asks them not to spread it until the carriage is out of sight. Maid: half the staff is from around here, east side especially; word will travel by tonight. Gatekeeper: "ma'am" (Gabriella) has been here two weeks and says plenty the staff hear. Gatekeeper has a cousin on the east side.

**Marchand Street, late morning.** Julian and Carla arrive by carriage, stopping short; Carla stays at the gate. Tomas Hollis, near eighteen, tall and thin, dirt to the wrist, trowel in hand, answers the blue door. Julian gives his name and says Bernard left him a key with Marguerite's name on it. Tomas lets him in, says wait in the hall, don't wander.

**Parlor, with Marguerite Hollis.** Present: Julian, Marguerite; Tomas in the doorway. Julian tells her of the twelve years of payments, the note "for the boy's schooling," and the key. Marguerite: the key is to Edmund's strongbox; the bank held it after Edmund died and wouldn't open it to her without a court; Bernard said he'd see it sorted. Whatever's inside belongs to Tomas, not her. Marchand Mutual held Edmund's accounts, but the strongbox went to the Islaport Trust when the old branch closed — needs a court order unless the paper names Julian. Julian admits he doesn't know whether the box is in his name or Tomas's; the will named him the fortune but no strongbox and no Tomas. Julian proposes: go to the Trust tomorrow with Tomas at his side; if paper is needed, ask what paper, and Julian pays for a lawyer. Tomas may keep the key and go alone if he prefers.

**Agreed:** Tomas keeps the key. Meet at the Islaport Trust at **one o'clock tomorrow (Day 2) afternoon** — mornings too busy at the clerk's window. Tomas brings the key; Julian brings the lawyer's name and whatever paper the will gave him. Tomas: his mother will spend tonight arguing him out of it — she's against hoping, not against Julian.

**Carriage back to the estate.** Carla: the boy is steady; Marguerite won't make a fuss about the key. Time enough for Jezebel to ring the bank and to put word out at the Trust that a Redmist will ask after Hollis by name tomorrow. Julian will watch the deputy, not the box. Carla: they need a lawyer who knows the Trust's rulebook — Jezebel will know who.

**Back at the estate, late morning.** Gatekeeper meets the carriage with a folded note from Jezebel, to be given the moment Julian returned. Staff were told about Marchand straight, as Julian asked.

**Jezebel's note (read by Julian and Carla):** the last payment cleared three weeks ago — after the funeral, before the will was read; sent on Bernard's standing, not Julian's. Not collected since, but the account was never formally closed; counsel not to close it yet — a closed account leaves a trail, an open one leaves a door. Lawyer: **Halvorsen**, old, methodical, has beaten the Trust's rulebook. Jezebel will ring him tonight; he'll want the will in hand and a client whose name he can say without flinching.

**Carla's plan:** watch the open account for a week to learn who signs the draughts now. Halvorsen to see Julian before one o'clock tomorrow, not at it.

**Lisette:** Campion rang; call back before the evening (after six he takes the receiver off). She shows Julian a study off the south corridor — dark paneling, fire lit, decanter, telephone on the desk; Bernard's old sitting room, unused since spring, sorted by Carla this morning.

**Campion call, late afternoon.** Present: Julian (desk), Carla (by the fire). Campion, brief: (1) the master-key handoff cannot wait past the week — **Friday, ten in the morning**, before offices open properly; bring only himself; the slip is in the vault, opened with Campion's key and Jezebel's. (2) Bernard altered his will in his last month — a codicil, six lines, names no one, grants nothing, speaks of a trust established years before, off the estate's books. Codicil text: "To my solicitor, my accountant, and my grandson. The trust established in the winter of the year of the Draught belongs to no name that appears on the estate's books. Its beneficiaries are known only to me and to my banker. No inquiry into it is to be treated as suspicious. It is only Bernard tidying." Campion knew nothing of it until he read it aloud this afternoon; has sat on it since three o'clock. The Draught = the winter the harbor froze, **1905**; the city still counts years from it. "My banker," singular: Bernard kept accounts at Marchand Mutual until it closed, then Islaport Trust; a man who leaves the oldest account open means the old door to still work. Codicil stays quiet; Halvorsen hears it from Julian, not Campion's clerk. Line clicked dead.

**Julian's decision:** tell Tomas about the codicil before the Trust, on the street, from paper not voice — his grandfather's hand is harder to argue with. Carla agrees; she'll take the codicil to Tomas tonight, have him at the Trust by half past twelve, bench opposite the door. If Tomas won't come out tonight, that's an answer too. Carla to send word to the estate the moment she knows whether the boy is coming. If Tomas asks why Julian didn't bring it himself, tell the truth.
