Treat this as conceptual and work-in-progress skill. Not as a prescription to act in some specific way. The user is this in progress of figuring this out.

Nuance in the codebase can cause or increase the amount of slop code being produced.

The more nuance in the codebase, the less predictable agents can act. This results in surprising (to the human) and unpredictable behaviour. 

What makes this complicated is the bitter lesson (Richard Sutton, 2019). Due to the bitter lesson, over prescribing behaviour (via skills and rules generally) can be counterproductive. 
Right now the most obvious things to do to reduce the nuance is to update the codebase in various ways, and to add guardrails to restrict behaviour.
